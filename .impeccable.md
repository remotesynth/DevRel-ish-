## Design Context

### Users
Developer relations professionals and adjacent roles (developer advocates, DevEx engineers, technical writers, community managers). Technical enough to be on Bluesky and care about ATProto, but fundamentally people-first. They browse the site during the day — at conferences, between meetings, on a laptop. Context is relaxed and exploratory, not high-stakes.

### Brand Personality
**Playful, open, nerdy.** Community-first, not corporate. Has opinions (it's on ATProto) but doesn't take itself too seriously. Warmth is a feature, not an accident. Should feel like a well-made indie conference program or a community zine — crafted, but not slick.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light (warm cream base — appropriate for daytime browsing)
- **Palette**: Terracotta primary, yellow secondary, green accent — keep the warmth, don't neutralize it
- **Feel**: Distinctive but grown-up. The display font should have character without being childish. Think editorial warmth rather than SaaS polish.
- **Anti-references**: Generic SaaS (Inter, blue CTAs, white backgrounds with subtle shadows). Over-designed startup (gradients, glassmorphism, hero illustrations with floating shapes).

### Design Principles
1. **Warmth over polish** — rough edges are fine; cold and corporate is not
2. **Character in every detail** — typography, color, and spacing should all feel chosen, not defaulted
3. **Community, not platform** — the UI should feel like a gathering place, not a product
4. **Open and legible** — this is for people who read; type clarity is non-negotiable
5. **Playful but grown-up** — humor and personality are welcome; childishness is not
